What a brief becomes. Once signed, a brief runs a loop no person sits in. A controller reads the needs and deliverables and, for each thing the map does not yet know, mints an unknown — one typed question (a number, a boolean, a label) citing the need or deliverable it comes from — and routes it as a task. A worker answers an unknown only one way: it writes a small probe that reads the source the need names, runs it, and the reading lands on the map; agreeing runs make it a known. A mechanical gate then checks the map against the brief — every need answered at confidence, every artifact a deliverable names covered by a known — and the loop is done when the gate is green. Nothing on the map is typed in by hand: the loop can only meet a need through readings a worker takes off a file, a page, data or an artifact. That is the controller's work — it decides which readings a need takes — and it is why a need should be about something: a page, a file, a result, a property of the work. A need about nothing a worker could ever read (pure taste, with no source and no artifact) leaves the controller with nothing to mint and stalls; when you see one, ask the Administrator what they would look at to judge it, and write that in.
