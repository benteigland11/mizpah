## Choosing the type

The type is how you drive the loop, not a label on the question.

- **number**, **boolean**, **label** — what a probe reads off a source. One reading, one source, no judgement in between. Mint these for what the work must produce and what can be read from it.
- **formula** — how a reading becomes a verdict. When the brief asks for a quantity to reach a target, sit inside a range, or stand in a proportion to another, the reading alone can never say so: it is a number, and a number is neither met nor missed. Mint the readings, then mint the formula over them that the brief's own words make true or false. A need with a threshold and no formula is a need nothing on the map is checking.
- **relation** — when what matters is how one quantity moves with another, not either alone. Readings are points along it.

The order is always the same: read first, compose second. A formula minted before the readings it names has nothing to stand on; readings minted without the formula that judges them leave the gate green on work that misses the brief.
