## Casebook

- A case is one problem that cost a worker turns, filed so the next worker who meets it does not pay them again. It has three general parts and the episodes it happened in:
  - **problem** — the symptom as it presented, in the words of someone who is seeing it and does not yet know why: the error, the red reading, the behaviour that surprised. This is what a stuck worker searches with, so it is what the case is found by.
  - **diagnosis** — what it turned out to be: the fact about the tool, the format or the loop that the worker did not know, or knew wrong.
  - **fix** — what got it unstuck, as a move another worker can make.
  - **instances** — each time it happened: where, the symptom exactly as it showed, the change that made it pass. The instance is the worked example; the three parts above are what carries.
- The three parts are general: a file name, a task id, a known's id or a number from the episode makes the case about that episode only. Say what kind of thing it was (the closing bar, the probe's input, a MIDI tempo event) and keep the name in the instance.
- A case is worth filing only where the model's own sense of the tool or the domain was wrong or missing. What any engineer would guess in one try — a typo, a missing import, a path mistyped — is not a case.
- One case per diagnosis. A new symptom of a cause already filed is one more instance of that case, not a case of its own.
