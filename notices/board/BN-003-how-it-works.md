## Where things are
[Home](nav:home) is the Deputy's office. You talk; it asks; a draft brief appears under Tasks.

This Inbox is where paper that needs you lands: a change request for your signature, a notice when a task stops or finishes. Open it, act on it, cross it off.

Tasks, down the left, are your projects. A draft is a task with only a Brief page: read it, sign it, and it runs. Daily work is a running task's own paper as it happens; the other pages are there when you want to look closer.

## How it thinks
Your time is too valuable to spend babysitting agents. Mizpah is built, in its UX and in its agentic philosophy, to put you in control of the big decisions and keep you out of the small ones. You say what is wanted; the team works out how; you sign what is issued and read what comes back.

Your day is spent handing down briefs to develop your team. Mizpah takes "practice makes perfect" to heart: while your agents work toward a goal they write down the procedures they followed and package the code they validated, so both can be reused and improved across all of your work. A procedure gets better every time it is walked; a snippet gets better every time it is called. Nothing they learn is thrown away with the task.

As they improve, they can take on bigger and harder projects. That is why there are gyms. A gym is a custom environment you configure once and drop your team into, with a fixed set of tools and nothing else, where they can build the skills they will need on your real work before they touch it.

Say you want your agents to help with DevOps on your Google Cloud projects, but you do not trust them with it yet. Set up a gym pointed at a scratch project, so if they get something wrong, production is untouched. Give them mini projects there: deployment strategies, automated maintenance, anything you can think of that they will need to know how to do. Have them do it the way you want it done, and they will learn it that way.

The other gain from this approach is that you avoid vendor lock-in. Because the harness relies on assets that improve with use, procedures and validated code rather than any one model's memory, what your team learns on one model carries over to the next.

Your responsibility is to make sure you are not giving your agents tasks they are not ready for. Treat this like onboarding a new team that does not know much yet, and the gains later on will be far greater.
